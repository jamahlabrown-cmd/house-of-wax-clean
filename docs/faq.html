For Buyers

Buying on House Of Wax

How does buying work?

Browse listings by artist or genre, or look through what's new. Message a seller directly if you have questions about condition, pressing details, or anything else — you're talking to the person who actually owns the item. When you're ready, check out.

What happens when I check out?

Checkout reserves the item immediately and starts a 5-day window to pay. Adding something to your cart doesn't hold it by itself — items from the same seller combine into one payment at checkout.

How do I pay, and is it safe?

You pay the seller directly through PayPal, and separately pay House Of Wax's platform fee, also through PayPal. House Of Wax never holds or processes your payment — it moves straight between you, the seller, and PayPal.

Are the photos on listings real?

Every live listing has to include a photo before it can be published. New listings can start from a labeled reference photo of that same pressing so the page isn't blank, and sellers are encouraged to add their own photos of the actual item — especially important for condition-sensitive or non-music items.

What should I check before buying?

Photos, condition notes, the seller's profile and trust standing, price, and shipping or pickup details. If anything's unclear, ask the seller directly before you check out.

For Sellers

Selling on House Of Wax

How do I get paid, and what does House Of Wax take?

You get paid directly by the buyer through PayPal — House Of Wax never holds your money. Buyers separately pay a platform fee to House Of Wax on top of your price, so you keep what you list it for. The exact fee percentage is shown in your seller dashboard before you publish anything.

How do I know what to price something at?

When you're setting or updating a price, House Of Wax shows a suggested price range for music items — pulled from real market data and comparable past sales on the platform. You always set the final price yourself.

Do I need my own photos of the item?

Yes, whenever possible — especially for condition-sensitive or non-music items. A reference photo can get a new listing started, but a real photo of the exact copy you're selling builds real buyer trust.

Why does my seller account need approval?

House Of Wax approves who can sell before they go live, and can review reports on listings afterward. It keeps the marketplace something buyers can actually trust.

What makes a strong listing?

A clear title, accurate category, honest condition notes, real photos, item identifiers (catalog number, pressing details), a fair price, and any flaws described upfront rather than left for the buyer to discover.

Getting started

Apply from your House Of Wax account, add your first listing, and you're live in your own store — no separate seller platform to learn.

Condition & Photos

What the condition grades mean

House Of Wax uses standard collector grading for both media (the record, disc, or item itself) and sleeve or packaging condition.

GradePlain-language meaning
Mint / Near MintUnused or almost flawless. Most used items are not truly Mint.
Very Good PlusPlayed but strong. May have light marks or sleeve scuffs, but no major playback problems.
Very GoodNoticeable wear. May have surface noise, marks, or visible use, but still collectible if described honestly.
Good+Well used but playable. More wear than Very Good, but not yet heavy damage.
GoodHeavy wear. Buyers should expect clear flaws or noise. Detailed notes matter.
Fair / PoorMajor flaws, damage, warps, skips, or heavy wear. Sold only with clear photos and direct notes.
Trust & Safety

How trust works here

Seller and buyer standing reflects completed transactions and real reviews — not a self-reported rating. House Of Wax reviews reports and can hide, restrict, or remove listings and sellers that don't meet marketplace standards. Counterfeit, stolen, unsafe, or misleading listings don't belong here.
